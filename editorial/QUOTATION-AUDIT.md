# Quotation audit — 8 September 2026

English Road's 4,200-item bank was screened across all 35 topics for missing quotation marks around words and phrases discussed as language. Candidate passages were reviewed individually, with additional passes over answer choices, questions, partly quoted explanations, contractions, and plural possessives. The review also covered the public pages, interface guidance, fallback quiz explanations, and copy-ready AI tutoring prompts.

## Changes

- Corrected 3,403 distinct passages in 1,769 items across 33 topics. Repeated correct-answer feedback brings this to 4,542 stored field occurrences.
- Quoted grammatical forms and vocabulary targets when they are named or explained, including embedded references within longer explanations and answer choices.
- Used double quotation marks for newly quoted expressions containing apostrophes and clarified four existing passages containing plural possessives.
- Added quotation marks around button names mentioned in help text, including the nine available help languages, and the “small sample” label in the methodology explanation.
- Added quotation guidance to the ready-to-copy AI tutoring prompts.

Examples:

- `Put not after is: is not, or isn't.` → `Put 'not' after 'is': 'is not', or "isn't".`
- `Have lunch is a common phrase...` → `'Have lunch' is a common phrase...`
- `Does is not shortened to 's...` → `'Does' is not shortened to "'s"...`

Ordinary uses of words within sentences remain unquoted. Conventional grammatical labels such as “to-infinitive” and “-ing form” do not receive unnecessary quotation marks throughout the bank.

Two explanations received small wording clarifications: the short-answer explanation now says that the question does not use 'have' and retains its 'be' verb; the confidentiality explanation now refers to the possible uncertainty in 'may not' without an awkward possessive construction. All other item changes preserve the original wording apart from quotation marks.
