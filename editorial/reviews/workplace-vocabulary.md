### 1. Job versus hotel visit

ID: `coverage-workplace-vocabulary-a1-4081`

Mina has a new job at a hotel. She starts on Monday.

What does Mina have?

1. a room she has booked
2. a holiday she has planned
3. a visit to meet a friend
4. work she will be paid to do **[Correct]**

**Explanation:** A job here is paid work at the hotel.

**Choice feedback:**

- **a room she has booked:** A guest books a room; a job is work, not accommodation.
- **a holiday she has planned:** Starting a job means starting work, not taking a holiday.
- **a visit to meet a friend:** A social visit is not a job.
- **work she will be paid to do:** A job here is paid work at the hotel.

**Decision:** replaced.

**Original findings:** Job was paired with unrelated person and payment definitions.

**Learning value:** Separates employment from being a hotel guest.

**Level rationale:** Short present-tense statements and familiar work suit A1.

**Similarity review:** Employment itself, not job applications.
