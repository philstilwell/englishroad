### 1. Full stop between independent statements

ID: `coverage-sentence-boundaries-a1-3361`

You work in a shop. Your sister is a driver. Keep both facts in two complete sentences.

Which version separates the two statements correctly?

1. I work in a shop my sister is a driver.
2. I work in a shop. My sister is a driver. **[Correct]**
3. I work. In a shop my sister is a driver.
4. I work in a shop, my sister is a driver.

**Explanation:** Each statement has its own subject and verb. A full stop separates them while keeping 'in a shop' with your work.

**Choice feedback:**

- **I work in a shop my sister is a driver.:** The second statement starts at 'my sister'; it needs a sentence boundary.
- **I work in a shop. My sister is a driver.:** Each statement has its own subject and verb. A full stop separates them while keeping 'in a shop' with your work.
- **I work. In a shop my sister is a driver.:** This moves 'in a shop' to the statement about your sister and loses the stated location of your work.
- **I work in a shop, my sister is a driver.:** A comma alone does not separate these two statements into sentences.

**Decision:** replaced.

**Original findings:** Mina/tired/so repeated across A1; shared-subject foil was not securely wrong and feedback was generic.

**Learning value:** Locates a boundary at the start of a new subject and predicate.

**Level rationale:** Two short present-tense statements use familiar jobs.

**Similarity review:** Work introduction with two distinct subjects, not causal coordination.
