### 1. Waking as ending sleep, not necessarily leaving bed

ID: `coverage-phrasal-verbs-a1-2401`

Read about Sam's morning.

Sam wakes up at six. What happens at six?

1. He leaves his bed.
2. He starts work.
3. He starts sleeping.
4. He stops sleeping. **[Correct]**

**Explanation:** Wake up means stop sleeping. It does not mean you leave bed immediately.

**Choice feedback:**

- **He leaves his bed.:** He may stay in bed after waking. Leaving bed is getting up.
- **He starts work.:** Waking is the end of sleep, not the start of a work shift.
- **He starts sleeping.:** Starting to sleep is falling asleep, the opposite change.
- **He stops sleeping.:** Wake up means stop sleeping. It does not mean you leave bed immediately.

**Decision:** replaced.

**Original findings:** The original had unrelated action foils and feedback copied from a definition list.

**Learning value:** Separates waking from the next morning action.

**Level rationale:** A short present-tense routine and common actions suit A1.

**Similarity review:** Only waking-versus-rising distinction in the band.
